Abstract

Segmenting anatomies and pathologies in 3D medical images underpins diagnosis, treatment planning, and disease monitoring. Yet dense 3D labels are among the scarcest data in medicine, and segmentation models trained on them generalize poorly beyond their training distribution. General-purpose Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are not trained to segment volumes, but they already know much of the anatomy and radiology a clinician describes in words. We present RadContour, a training-free pipeline that turns a frozen MLLM into a text-prompted 3D segmenter of CT, MRI, and PET volumes, drawing outlines on slices, much as radiologists contour a lesion. The MLLM is the only vision component. It plans the target from a free-text prompt, localizes it in 3D from coarse to fine, and traces closed outlines on a few keyframes. Deterministic geometry then assembles the outlines into a 3D mask.

We evaluate RadContour zero-shot on 17 public CT, MRI, and PET benchmarks spanning 923 volumes. On nine tumor and lesion benchmarks, RadContour matches the mean Dice of the trained state of the art on identical cases (58.7 vs. 58.4) and more than doubles that of every other trained text-promptable model. On soft-tissue sarcoma, it nearly doubles the Dice of the best trained model (76.5 vs. 39.3). Across 25 backbone MLLMs, accuracy follows backbone strength with an unchanged pipeline.

Why Not Segment Slice by Slice?

A 2D MLLM segmenter (LLMSeg) run on every axial slice sees each slice alone. It detects bone where there is none, mistakes the femoral heads for hip bone, and its outlines jump between slices. RadContour, with the same MLLM (Haiku 5.5), decides what and where first, then traces a few keyframes.

How It Works

Four stages, with the MLLM as the only vision component. It never sees voxels or features: each call gets rendered 2D views with captions and returns JSON (boxes or point lists). Step through the real records of one case below (BTCV, prompt “spleen”).

Planning the target from text. The MLLM receives the prompt, the modality and three orthogonal overview slices, and returns an explicit plan: display window, appearance, location, size, confounders, multiplicity and shape class. The plan sets the sampling density of the later stages and is quoted in every later prompt.

Coarse-to-fine localization. A coarse call sees sparse axial views (plus
            coronal and sagittal context) and boxes the target wherever it appears, which gives an initial extent.
            A fine call then sees dense slices over that extent and returns one box per slice. These boxes fix
            the extent and the in-plane region on every slice.

Fine call: dense slices over the extent, one box each

              

            

          

        


        
        

          
Keyframe contouring. Each keyframe is shown as a pair: the FULL slice with the
            crop outlined (to recognize the target in context) and a zoomed CROP (to trace it). The MLLM answers
            “absent” or returns closed loops of points; a closed interpolating cubic spline passes through them.
            Probe slices beyond each end let the extent grow.

          

            
            
          

          

            

          

          

        


        
        

          

            

              
3D assembly. Each keyframe mask becomes a signed distance map; between
                keyframes the maps are blended linearly and the negative region is the mask. Where a present keyframe
                neighbors an absent one, the mask ends halfway. A training-free intensity snapping step then moves
                boundary voxels within a thin band to the side (inside or outside) whose intensity statistics fit them
                better.

              

                φ(u, z) = (1 − λ) φk(u) + λ φk+1(u),
                  M̃ = [φ < 0]

Frozen MLLM, nothing trained

              
No segmentation network, no labeled volumes, no fine-tuning. Swap the backbone and the pipeline stays

Contours like a radiologist

              
Find the target while scrolling, check it in other views, draw the boundary on selected slices and
                leave the slices in between to interpolation.

            

          

        

        

          

            
            

              
Geometry keeps 3D consistent

              
Neighboring slices agree because signed distance blending, not the MLLM, fills the gaps between
                keyframes.

            

          

        

        

          

            
            

              
Inspectable at every stage

              
Plan, boxes and outlines are JSON. Every prompt and reply is stored, so all masks can be rebuilt
                without API calls.

What We Learned

        

      

      

        

          

            
82.0 vs 48.7

            
Recognition is the bottleneck

            
The MLLM traces what it recognizes. A trained segmenter reaches 82.0 Dice with ground-truth boxes but
              48.7 with the MLLM's boxes; only 20% of the lost Dice comes from imperfect outlines inside a correct box.

          

        

        

          

            
< 1 point

            
A few traced slices suffice

            
Tracing only every second keyframe (about 7 mm between traced slices instead of 3.4 mm) loses less than
              one Dice point; accuracy falls quickly beyond 14 mm. Three times fewer keyframes save over a third of the
              calls for about one point.

          

        

        

          

            
+2.4

            
Snapping is free accuracy

            
Moving boundary voxels to the side whose intensity statistics fit them adds about 2.4 Dice on anatomy
              with no extra MLLM call.

          

        

        

          

            
$0.14 / $1.5

            
Cost per volume

            
Summed over all MLLM calls, with Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5. The marginal cost of a new concept
              or modality is a new prompt, not a new labeled dataset and training run.

          

        

      

      
RadContour still trails trained segmenters on small lesions and on small
        or thin organs, is slower than a single network pass, and relies on commercial APIs whose models change over
        time. Sending patient images to an external API must comply with local data-protection rules.
